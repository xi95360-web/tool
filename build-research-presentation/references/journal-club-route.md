# Journal Club Route

## Purpose

Do more than summarize a paper. Reconstruct its argument, test whether each conclusion follows from the experiments, compare it with the field, and end with a defensible verdict.

## Recommended Narrative

1. **Title / one-line verdict** — paper identity plus the central question or provisional verdict.
2. **Why this paper now** — scientific relevance, not journal prestige.
3. **Minimum background** — only concepts required to interpret the experiments.
4. **Prior evidence and unresolved gap** — cite original work where practical.
5. **Authors' question, hypothesis, and predictions**.
6. **Study design map** — samples/models, interventions, readouts, and figure sequence.
7. **Result block 1** — one claim and the evidence that tests it.
8. **Result block 2**.
9. **Result block 3**.
10. **Mechanistic or integrative model** — distinguish authors' model from demonstrated facts.
11. **Evidence synthesis** — which conclusions are strong, partial, or unsupported.
12. **Alternative explanations and missing controls**.
13. **Consistency or conflict with independent literature**.
14. **Reproducibility, generalizability, and practical significance**.
15. **Verdict** — answer the opening question with calibrated confidence.
16. **Most informative next experiment**.
17. **Discussion questions**.
18. **Backup** — full methods, extra panels, statistical details, related papers, and likely questions.

Before selecting the visible panels, map the paper's complete argument:

| Authors' claim | Main figure/panel | Relevant supplement | Included in talk? | If excluded, why? | Presenter's verdict |
|---|---|---|---:|---|---|
|  |  |  | yes/no |  | strong/partial/unsupported |

“Main” means every figure or supplemental result necessary for a claim highlighted in the abstract, title, discussion, graphical model, or presenter verdict. A 15-minute talk may omit panels, but it may not silently omit evidence that weakens or qualifies the verdict.

## Figure-by-Figure Protocol

For every discussed figure or panel:

1. State the question being tested.
2. Explain the design before showing the result.
3. Identify model, intervention, comparator, control, readout, sample size, and analysis.
4. Orient the audience to axes, groups, colors, and panels.
5. Point to the decisive observation.
6. State the narrow conclusion supported.
7. Identify an alternative explanation or limitation.
8. Explain why the next experiment logically follows.

Do not narrate panels in publication order if a clearer logical order exists.

## Critical Appraisal

Evaluate:

- construct validity: does the assay measure the intended phenomenon?
- internal validity: are confounding, bias, controls, and analysis handled?
- causal strength: perturbation, rescue, dose/time logic, and orthogonal evidence;
- statistical strength: effect size, uncertainty, multiplicity, independence, and missing data;
- external validity: models, populations, conditions, and boundary cases;
- reproducibility: preregistration, data/code/material availability, validation, and independent support;
- interpretation: whether claims exceed data;
- novelty: what is genuinely new relative to prior evidence.

Separate criticism from consequence. State whether a limitation is fatal, reduces confidence, narrows scope, or merely motivates follow-up.

## Reference Roles

A strong journal club normally includes:

- the focal paper;
- one orienting review;
- two or more primary papers establishing the gap;
- a method or benchmark paper when needed;
- at least one independent, conflicting, or boundary-setting paper.

The number is flexible; relevance and traceability matter more than citation volume.

## Discussion Questions

Prefer questions with scientific consequences:

- Which result is necessary but not sufficient for the authors' model?
- What alternative mechanism fits the same data?
- Which missing control would most change confidence?
- What experiment best distinguishes the competing explanations?
- Which conclusion is likely to generalize, and where might it fail?

Avoid questions answerable by reading a label already visible on the slide.
